Track 1 · Health4 files · 15,012 rows

The Forgotten Patient

Build a hospital-flow product that helps staff understand patient journeys, identify likely bottlenecks, and make better-informed operational decisions.

Built for

Hospital operations managersDepartment coordinatorsService administrators

What you could build

  • A patient-flow dashboard with department-level queues and wait times.
  • A forecast of demand or likely delays by department/time period.
  • Alerts for unusual waiting patterns or potentially overloaded services.
  • A diagnostic-test turnaround monitor.
  • A “what should we investigate first?” queue ranked by impact and confidence.
  • An explanation panel showing which data signals led to each alert.
Full problem brief

A patient’s experience in a hospital is a connected journey: arrival, triage, consultation, diagnostic tests, treatment, and discharge. When these stages are managed separately, delays and capacity issues can be difficult to spot early. Hospital teams need a clearer view of where patients are waiting, which services are under pressure, and where intervention may help.

How the data helps

Teams can link visits to patients and departments, calculate wait and service patterns, compare departments, estimate test turnaround, study time-of-day demand, and build an operational view from several tables. This data is simulated and is not suitable for clinical diagnosis.

Possible ML / data-science work

Waiting-time regression, demand forecasting, clustering of visit patterns, anomaly detection, prioritization/ranking, or queue/resource optimization. Use a time-aware split when evaluating predictions involving future time.

Useful product question

“What is likely to get delayed next, why, and what should the operations team examine?”

Track 2 · Education4 files · 11,920 rows

The Campus Has Space. Students Don’t.

Build a campus-space product that helps students find suitable rooms and helps administrators use existing campus space more effectively.

Built for

StudentsClub coordinatorsFacultyCampus facilities administrators

What you could build

  • A room finder that matches time, capacity, accessibility, and facilities.
  • A room-availability estimate using the class schedule and room requests.
  • A utilization dashboard showing where space is consistently underused.
  • A recommendation engine for room allocation or event relocation.
  • A demand forecast for busy days/time periods.
  • An explanation for each match (“capacity fit, projector available, no clash at that time”).
Full problem brief

Universities may have classrooms, laboratories, seminar rooms, and study spaces while students and clubs still struggle to find a suitable place at the right time. Some rooms may be underused, while others are in high demand. Fixed schedules and fragmented booking information make it hard to match actual demand to available capacity and facilities.

How the data helps

Room metadata defines capacity and facilities; schedules describe planned use; requests represent demand; event records add non-classroom demand. Together, these allow teams to estimate utilization, rank room matches, and suggest better allocations.

Possible ML / data-science work

Availability classification, demand forecasting, room recommendation/ranking, clustering of utilization patterns, or constrained room allocation. A valid solution should account for capacity, facilities, timing, and conflicting bookings — not merely predict availability.

Useful product question

“Given a room request, what is the best feasible room and what evidence supports that recommendation?”

Track 3 · Smart City3 files · 19,000 rows

Where Should We Fix First?

Build a city-prioritization product that helps officials identify problem locations, understand the evidence behind their ranking, and compare possible interventions.

Built for

Municipal operations teamsRoad/traffic departmentsCivic-service coordinators

What you could build

  • A map and ranked list of locations requiring attention.
  • A complaint/incident trend monitor.
  • A severity-and-frequency-based priority score.
  • A resolution-time dashboard for public complaints.
  • A recommendation queue with reasons and confidence, not an unexplained “risk score.”
Full problem brief

Cities receive reports about road damage, traffic, flooding, streetlights, drainage, and pedestrian safety. Budgets and field teams are limited, so not every issue can be handled at once. Authorities need a transparent way to combine incident history, public complaints, traffic conditions, and local context to decide which locations deserve attention first.

How the data helps

The common location_id allows incidents, complaints, road conditions and context to be brought together. Teams can aggregate repeated issues, estimate patterns over time, compare zones, and create a transparent intervention-priority list.

Possible ML / data-science work

Incident-volume forecasting, complaint-category classification, resolution-time estimation, anomaly detection, location ranking, or resource-allocation optimization. Explain limitations and avoid claiming the synthetic coordinates represent real locations.

Useful product question

“Which locations should be reviewed first, based on what evidence, and what information is still missing?”

Track 4 · Business5 files · 27,450 rows

The Money Leak

Build a business decision-support product that helps a manager identify potential revenue or operational problems, investigate their likely drivers, and decide what to do next.

Built for

Business ownersRetail managersProduct/category managersOperations teams

What you could build

  • A revenue, cancellation, and return investigation dashboard.
  • A customer/product/region breakdown of performance.
  • A stockout and inventory-risk monitor.
  • A campaign-efficiency view connecting spend, clicks, and conversions.
  • A forecast or alert for unusual performance changes.
  • A “why this is flagged” panel with supporting metrics.
Full problem brief

A business can record thousands of transactions and still struggle to understand why revenue or margin is underperforming. Cancellations, returns, discounts, stockouts, customer changes, and marketing spend can tell different parts of the story. These signals are often spread across separate systems, making it difficult to turn raw records into a clear action.

How the data helps

Transaction records can be joined with customer and product information to study sales and status patterns. Inventory records add stock availability; campaigns add marketing context. Build a product around investigation and action rather than a generic sales chart.

Possible ML / data-science work

Cancellation/return classification, demand forecasting, customer segmentation, anomaly detection, stockout-risk estimation, campaign conversion analysis, or inventory optimization. Document assumptions about costs, prices, returns, and profit calculations.

Useful product question

“What appears to be going wrong, what evidence supports that finding, and what action should the manager consider?”
